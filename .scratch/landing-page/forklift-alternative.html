A lighter ForkLift alternative

If you need remote access, ForkLift 4 is still the right answer and nothing here beats it. SFTP, FTP, WebDAV, S3, Backblaze B2, Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox, SMB, AFP, NFS, plus folder sync and multi-rename — that is a genuinely deep feature set for a one-time $19.95.

People look for an alternative when they realise they never use any of it. If your files live on local and mounted drives, you are carrying a 16.3 MB remote-transfer suite to move things between two folders. The lighter native options are Marta (free), Nimble Commander (free, open source) and Diptychon (1.5 MB, pre-launch).

Questions people actually ask

Is ForkLift a subscription?

Not in the Path Finder sense — there is no recurring charge to keep using it. As verified on the vendor's store on 2026-07-11, a single licence is a one-time $19.95. Note the caveat above: BinaryNights' model bundles a limited window of updates with the licence, so a much later major version may need a new purchase. That is materially different from a subscription, and materially different from "buy once, updated forever".

What is a free ForkLift alternative?

Marta (free, not open source) and Nimble Commander (free and open source, with a $29.99 one-time Pro tier for admin/root operations). Nimble Commander is the one to look at if you need the remote side, since it can mount remote locations; Marta is local-only.

Does ForkLift have undo?

Partially. Its release notes document undo for Trash and Multi-Rename operations. The manual does not document undo at all, so the full extent is unclear. We deliberately do not claim ForkLift "has no undo" — it demonstrably has some.

Diptychon's undo is multi-level and spans move, copy, trash and rename, with overwrites excluded by design because they destroy the original.

Is ForkLift keyboard-driven?

Partly. ForkLift has shortcuts, but the workflow it is designed around is mouse-forward — drag between panels, click through remote connections. That is a reasonable design for a transfer tool. It is a different thing from a tool where every action has a binding and the mouse is optional, which is what Marta, Nimble Commander and Diptychon are.

What does Diptychon give up compared to ForkLift?

A lot, and mostly on purpose. No remote protocols, no folder sync, no cloud-storage integration — that is a deliberate boundary, not a gap waiting to be filled, and it is the reason the download is 1.5 MB instead of 16.3 MB. Archive handling is the one genuinely open item: not in v1, parked until the people signing up ask for it.

What you get back: every action on a key binding, multi-level undo across move, copy, trash and rename, real Finder tags, and no telemetry. Diptychon is pre-1.0 and under active development, so the feature list will grow — but it will grow inside that boundary, not across it. Today the honest comparison is still "ForkLift, which you can buy" against "a mailing list".

Look for a ForkLift alternative if…

  • You have never opened its remote features.
  • You want the mouse to be optional.
  • You want undo across everything, not some things.
  • The footprint bothers you on principle.

Keep ForkLift if…

  • You transfer to SFTP, S3 or WebDAV.
  • You rely on folder sync.
  • You want archive handling built in.
  • You need it today.
